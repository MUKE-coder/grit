Three numbers, and tell them which one to care about, because they will instinctively care about the second. Sixteen thousand rows a second is a fine number and it is not the point. The point is that the ceiling is a property of the pipeline rather than of how many rows you asked for: rows are built in chunks, written in batches, and the number of batches in flight is capped by a channel. The practical reason this exists is D9. A list page with ten rows is a lie. You find your pagination bug, your missing index and your N+1 at a million rows, in a minute, before a client does.
